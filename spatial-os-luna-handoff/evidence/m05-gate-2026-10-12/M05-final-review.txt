   world_remote_keys includes shared state, population, movement, restoration, and occlusion checks.
3. Admission separate from workspace and action authority: passed.
   Public admission accepts a registered actor without implicit grants or workspace selection.
   Explicit delegation permits shared actions. Private admission remains separately denied.
   Full local gates retain bootstrap, authority, and revocation regression suites.
4. Stable identity and persistent state: passed.
   The topology scenario restarts all three authorities and checks saved identities and contents.
   Shared-state scenarios verify saved room and pose and reject stale authentication proofs.
   Supervised recovery preserves committed edits and discards previews and abandoned leases after SIGKILL.
5. Authority failure and recovery: passed.
   Shared-authority shutdown produces local and remote transport loss and a closed endpoint.
   Both private worlds remain responsive during that outage.
   Direct crash recovery cannot bypass generation retirement.
   Supervised recovery uses actual fencing, dependent cleanup, issuer retirement, and fresh remote authentication.
   An unknown inherited listener descriptor cannot activate a replacement authority.
6. Separate actor keys and production transport: passed.
   Remote actor keys stay on their originating host with mode 600.
   Production spatial-key signs protobuf requests. Independent OpenSSL verification checks the signatures.
   Only public keys and proofs return to the trusted fixture controller.
   SSH provisions fixtures; it is not an actor execution capability.
