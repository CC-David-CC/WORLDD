Primary review: native cleanup before broker settlement

Confirmed failure

The failure-02 sb16 run used frozen candidate05 Debug binaries and its 252-file manifest.
The issuer reported pending_count=0 and complete=true while the owned X server was stopped.
The broker then exited with its native authority channel unavailable.
The test used a real issuer, delayed keepalive response, application namespace, and native credential.
All five observed descendant processes had exited before the completion query.
No mocked issuer state, simulated cleanup reply, or production source mutation was used.
The original test hash matches scripts-01/test_native_settlement_schedule.py.

Scheduling limits

The diagnostic attached GDB only to the test-owned broker.
It used non-stop mode and breakpoints. It did not write production program variables.
The first debugger attempt detached early and did not expose the failure.
The failing second attempt did not set its intended sustained-pause marker.
Its breakpoint processing still perturbed scheduling. Do not call it a deterministic held-thread witness.
The simpler relay-only runs passed on both hosts.
A later attempt to enable the boundary breakpoint after descendant exit also passed on both hosts.
That attempt did not reach its pause marker. These passes do not invalidate the recorded failure.

Root cause and correction

NativeLaunch::revoke closes its channel when native credential cleanup cannot be confirmed.
The broker checked that channel before reconciliation and process reaping.
Those operations can destroy a NativeCredential and lose its native cleanup reply.
The same loop iteration could submit an issuer acknowledgement before its next native channel check.
Worker scheduling could deliver that acknowledgement before broker failure stopped the worker.

Candidate06 checks the native channel again after reconciliation, reaping, and control updates.
No native cleanup operation occurs between that final check and settlement in the authority thread.
A lost cleanup reply now throws before the iteration can enqueue its acknowledgement.
The shutdown path also invokes this checked reconciliation before participant release.
Nonnative brokers retain their existing path. Successful native operations retain their existing replies.
The change does not modify the X server, its protocol, or the issuer schema.

One revokes directly. One delays a real keepalive response across revocation.
Both require pending completion after kernel process exit and after broker exit.
They isolate credential cleanup by releasing the fixture's control reservation before revocation.
They do not provide independent held-input evidence; the earlier world crash witnesses provide that boundary evidence.
