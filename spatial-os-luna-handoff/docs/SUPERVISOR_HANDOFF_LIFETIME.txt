Supervisor descriptor handoff lifetime
Status: Isolated handoff01 draft. Both new checks remain unrun.

Invariant

After successful child exec, the supervisor releases its copies of selected descriptors and the child's standard input and output.
Keep the supervisor's standard descriptor slots occupied by /dev/null.
Keep management, stderr, process ownership, and native ownership intact.

Source finding

AuthorityLaunch duplicates selected descriptors into the owned child.
The supervisor retains the original selected descriptors after that handoff.
It also retains the child stdin and stdout streams.
These copies can prevent a peer from receiving EOF when the child dies.
A world endpoint retained by its supervisor can delay broker channel fencing.
This finding requires the independent runtime reproducer before integration.

Correction

Release the selected source copies after successful child start.
Replace supervisor stdin and stdout with /dev/null using dup2.
Do not shutdown the shared socket endpoint. The child's references remain valid.
Keep slots 0 and 1 occupied because private descriptor helpers require descriptors above stderr.
Retain stderr for failure diagnostics.
Do not transfer or close the supervisor's management channel or native ownership handles.
No protocol schema or locked acceptance file changes.

Independent checks

Run a real production supervisor, issuer, native server, and owned participant child.
Wait for the child's real activation.
Pin the supervisor and child with pidfds.
Stop only that disposable supervisor and confirm its kernel stopped state.
Kill only its pinned child and confirm child exit through the retained pidfd.
Observe kernel EOF on the selected endpoint and child stdout before resuming the supervisor.
The preceding implementation should retain both endpoints and fail the two EOF assertions.
Resume the supervisor in every cleanup path.
Then check its actual process and native cleanup records.

Evidence limits

These checks prove descriptor-reference lifetime, not native cancellation or reservation cleanup.
The supervisor still owns checked process cleanup and native revocation.
The broker still preserves fenced reservations until selective recovery is verified.
Do not issue an issuer-retirement receipt from EOF alone.
