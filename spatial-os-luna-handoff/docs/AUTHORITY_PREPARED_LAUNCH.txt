Prepared authority launch
Status: isolated candidate01 passed focused Debug and runtime sanitizer qualification on both hosts.
Full combined qualification and production integration remain pending.

Invariant

Prepare and identify the owned process scope before authority execution.
Start the authority once, with only explicitly selected streams and inherited descriptors.
Do not expose supervisor credentials, pidfds, cgroup descriptors, or startup gates through accidental inheritance.

Purpose

The supervisor must know the process launch identity before it issues native authority credentials.
It must persist the coordinated ownership record before releasing authority execution.
The existing combined process launcher cannot expose that preparation boundary.
This change supplies the boundary. It does not implement the durable coordinator itself.

Private API and protobuf fields

ProcessScope::prepare creates and retains an empty scope without forking an authority.
ProcessScope::start validates the launch and releases one owned child through the existing startup gate.
Keep the existing launch convenience method as prepare followed by start.
Both paths use the same production implementation.

Add repeated uint32 inherited_fds at field 3 of AuthorityProcessLaunch.
Each element specifies the child descriptor for the corresponding descriptor in the private out-of-band table.
Require exactly one table entry per target. Allow at most sixteen entries.
Targets must be unique and between 3 and 255 inclusive.
Standard streams remain the three separately selected descriptors.
The private supervisor supplies the descriptor table. Actor requests cannot select supervisor descriptors.

Add bool started at field 4 of ProcessScopeState.
Add ProcessScopeQuery prepare at field 13 of ProcessScopeRequest.
Add AuthorityProcessLaunch start at field 14 of ProcessScopeRequest.
Preserve every existing field and its meaning.
A prepared scope reports no population, no started child, and no exited child.
A stopped scope cannot start. A scope cannot start twice.
After an uncertain fork or exec attempt, require explicit cleanup. Do not retry execution automatically.
The started field records the one-shot attempt. It does not prove service readiness or issuer activation.

Descriptor handling

Duplicate selected descriptors above the allowed target range before fork.
Keep the child startup gate outside that target range too.
Mark all nonstandard descriptors close-on-exec before assigning selected targets.
Assign selected targets from the retained copies. Do not overwrite another source during assignment.
Preserve the parent's descriptor table.
Unselected inherited descriptors must be closed by exec, including descriptors deliberately exposed by a test fixture.

Qualification

Verify the prepared scope through the real cgroup filesystem before any child exists.
Verify unchanged launch identity after start and actual child membership before its first recorded action.
Verify stop before start, rejection of a second start, and rejection after failed exec.
Pass two real sealed memfds to low child descriptors and verify exact contents.
Pass an additional unselected memfd to the probe and verify that the authority cannot access it.
Reject invalid, duplicate, excessive, and mismatched descriptor targets before executing authority code.
Keep all existing process-scope cleanup and kernel-denial checks unchanged.
Run Debug and runtime sanitizers on both hosts before integration.

Both hosts passed eight new checks and ten unchanged process-scope checks in each profile.
Each profile also passed 51 harness checks. No check failed or skipped.
All 265 source hashes and 41 locked hashes match.
See evidence/authority-prepared-launch-2026-10-09 for the original failures, patch, logs, and review.

Limits

This boundary does not persist launch records or authorize issuer retirement.
It does not copy supervisor secrets into workspace processes.
Durable coordinator recovery, dependent broker reservation cleanup, and signed fencing receipts remain required.
