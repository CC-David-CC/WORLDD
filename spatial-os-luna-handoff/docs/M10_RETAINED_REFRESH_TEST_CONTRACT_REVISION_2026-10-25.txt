Exact contradiction

The 2026-10-24 draft required a new participant progress receipt after each motion ingress.
The 2026-10-25 contract removes that barrier so an authenticated actor can refresh movement
while rendering, broker work, or another actor's authentication is slow. `queue_progress()`
requests the initial receipt only. The two old world-participant tests armed a fault after
the initial receipt and then waited for another progress command. Both waits were for a
request that the current contract deliberately removed.

The R9 Debug runs reproduced the mismatch on sb16 and sb16-2. CTest test #90,
`world-participant`, failed in both runs. The exact failing cases were
`test_failed_retained_check_cancels_active_motion_and_stays_disabled` and
`test_failed_retained_checks_never_apply_or_fall_back`. Preserve the full host logs and
source pins with the R9 attempt records. The failures do not establish a revocation-path
failure.

Neither test module nor either test ID appears in `acceptance.lock.json`. This revision
does not edit the lock.

Corrected invariant

Retained movement requires one valid initial participant proof. The world checks its
identity, service incarnation, activation generation, feed cursor, and sequence. No
movement may use the ordinary dispatch path while that proof is missing or invalid.

After the initial proof, an authenticated actor may refresh only current movement intent.
Each refresh still checks the actor session, token, sequence, navigation grant, live
participant, checked revocation feed, and target generation. Refresh does not request a new
participant progress receipt or wait for an issuer, broker, or renderer reply. The world
consumes feed events before applying queued refreshes. It cancels movement and held input
when it consumes a matching event. Participant loss, a bad feed, stale incarnation, bad
refresh, or sequence failure disables the path and closes the actor channel. There is no
fallback to ordinary dispatch. Unrefreshed movement still expires after 250 ms.

For retained refresh, an issuer revocation commit is not itself evidence that the world
received the corresponding feed event. Until delivery, the world acts on its latest
checked feed. The strict default-off checked-dispatch test continues to require a direct
current-status check. The retained-mode test now records this distinct ordering: refreshes
continue while the revocation event is held, then stop when the event is delivered and
consumed. This is the explicit feed boundary in the 2026-10-25 contract.

Why the correction does not weaken authorization

The initial proof remains mandatory. Every refresh uses the same authenticated actor
session and navigation authority. No caller-controlled identity, stale target, malformed
sequence, or unchecked feed can authorize movement. A consumed revocation cancels the
actor before later refreshes apply. Participant or feed failure closes the channel and
releases motion without waiting for the 250 ms intent expiry. The old direct-status test
remains pinned to checked-dispatch mode. The new retained-mode case separately verifies
the liveness ordering and cancellation after feed delivery.

Counterexamples retained or added

- Invalid initial generation, cursor, or replayed progress sequence cannot enable motion.
- A refresh sent before the initial proof is accepted cannot move the actor or fall back.
- Losing the checked participant after motion starts closes the actor channel and stops
  motion before the ten-second fixture expiry.
- A held revocation event leaves the issuer barrier pending. Retained refresh remains
  live during that interval, then movement stops after the event is consumed.
- Existing renderer, broker, and second-actor authentication regressions continue to
  cover movement progress during unrelated slow operations.
