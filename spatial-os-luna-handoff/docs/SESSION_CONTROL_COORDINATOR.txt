Session control coordinator
Status: Partial production implementation. SessionBroker owns the supervised broker. World coordination remains incomplete.

Invariant

The session retains broker management independently of every world launch.
Each world endpoint maps to one retained, authorized compositor launch.
A world failure closes only that world's control path.
It does not close broker management or stop independently authorized applications.

Existing implementation boundaries

src/platform/broker.cpp provides BrokerProcess and BrokerClient.
BrokerProcess starts workspaced directly. It does not provide supervised authority launch ownership.
src/platform/worker.cpp uses that path for the conformance host.
Do not promote that fixture launch into the production session coordinator.

src/supervisor/main.cpp supervises one authorized process launch.
It retains process scope, native scope, participant connection, and launch authorization.
Its private parent channel supports lifecycle operations.
The binding01 candidate adds a control_owner query derived from those retained handles.
That query does not activate a generation or prove cleanup.

The owner04 broker candidate accepts separate root, administrative, and world channels.
Root EOF still shuts down workspaced and its applications.
Administrative failure fences admitted world channels but preserves reservations and application execution.
World-channel failure affects only its immutable owner.

Required process and descriptor ownership

Add one production session coordinator above the existing per-launch supervisors.
Keep the broker supervisor independent of all world supervisors.
Launch the broker through the existing authorized supervisor path.
Retain the broker root channel in the session coordinator.
Create the broker administrative socket pair in that same coordinator process.
Pass only the broker endpoint through its supervisor into workspaced.
Configure the administrative peer as the coordinator, not the intermediary supervisor.
Keep the administrative sender endpoint in the coordinator for its complete lifetime.

Create each world socket pair in the coordinator.
Pass the world endpoint only to the corresponding retained world supervisor and child.
Close unused endpoint copies after their checked handoff.
Never pass root or administrative channels to a world, compositor, actor, or application.
Keep all descriptors close-on-exec except explicitly mapped child descriptors.
Keep the existing supervisor checks against privileged descriptor aliases.

Kernel socket credentials identify the socket creator.
They do not identify a later process that inherits the endpoint.
The coordinator therefore owns the endpoint-to-launch mapping.
Do not infer that mapping from a caller's PID, world name, or serialized owner fields.
Do not relax the broker's per-packet sender check to permit another sender.

World admission sequence

1. Retain the exact world supervisor process and its private management connection.
2. Start its authorized world launch with the dedicated world endpoint.
3. Read the initial launch record from that retained supervisor.
4. Query control_owner through the same private connection.
5. Validate the returned binding against that launch record and expected issuer.
6. Register the paired broker endpoint with that exact owner.
7. Validate the complete registration reply before reporting control-channel readiness.

The world may queue its initial broker request before registration.
Do not report readiness from process creation or a queued request alone.
Registration does not replace actor, application, or workspace authorization.
Do not accept a caller-selected owner or an unrelated inherited endpoint.

Failure handling

Correlate every supervisor and broker reply with its request and expected operation.
Validate protobuf messages recursively, including versions and nested fields.
Bound packet sizes, pending requests, and response deadlines.
Treat timeout, malformed replies, and lost registration replies as uncertain effects.
Inspect the exact owner when the administrative connection remains usable.
Do not retry registration with a different identity to bypass uncertainty.

On world failure, fence its broker channel before accepting replacement control work.
Then obtain checked process and native cleanup through the retained supervisor.
Keep unrelated worlds and application execution active.
Retain reservations until the separate selective recovery operation verifies cleanup.
Never infer cleanup from EOF, process exit, or elapsed time alone.

Coordinator failure must fence managed world channels through the existing broker transport rules.
Do not promise application survival after coordinator or broker failure.
Reopening a journal cannot recreate lost live handles or authorize replacement execution.
Record unresolved ownership and require the existing checked recovery path.

Administrative client implementation requirements

The coordinator creates and owns the administrative socket pair.
SO_PEERCRED therefore identifies its creator, not the supervised broker child.
Pin the exact retained broker child with a pidfd before accepting replies.
Require SCM_CREDENTIALS with that child PID and the configured UID on every reply.
Enable SO_PASSCRED before the broker can send its first reply.
Reject missing, duplicate, or mismatched credentials.
Reject reply descriptors and close every received descriptor, including malformed packets.
Reject MSG_TRUNC and MSG_CTRUNC before protobuf interpretation.
Use recvmsg with MSG_CMSG_CLOEXEC.
Use MSG_NOSIGNAL for sends.

Permit one administrative request in flight.
Use a bounded deadline for both sending and receiving.
Check broker process liveness while waiting.
Send one SCM_RIGHTS descriptor only for registration.
Registration uses the coordinator's exact endpoint-to-retained-launch mapping.
Inspect and fence send no descriptors.
Call validate_control_reply before interpreting any response.
A validated remote error remains an error.
A transport failure after sending leaves the operation uncertain.
Do not synthesize successful registration, fencing, cleanup, or retirement.
Do not replay an uncertain registration with another identity.

Its packet tests do not prove the coordinator's endpoint-to-launch mapping.
The private handle server and coordinator receiver are implemented on the development branch.
Do not report the complete recovery path as implemented from these component checks.

Broker ownership implementation, 2026-10-10

SessionBroker starts the real authorized supervisor and retains its kernel process handle.
It creates the root, administrative, lifecycle, and handle-transfer channels in the coordinator process.
Only explicit child endpoints survive supervisor exec.
ChildHandleClient verifies the supervisor sender, exact launch record, and one live direct-child pidfd.
BrokerClient and ControlAdmin duplicate that retained broker handle.
They do not reopen a broker PID on this production path.
The session validates checked process and native cleanup before accepting explicit shutdown.
Neither EOF nor shutdown acknowledges issuer retirement.

The library is production code. The probe only drives it with real supervisor and broker processes.
The new tests cover authenticated broker ownership and rejection paths.
They do not yet prove world endpoint registration, selective recovery, or application survival after world failure.
