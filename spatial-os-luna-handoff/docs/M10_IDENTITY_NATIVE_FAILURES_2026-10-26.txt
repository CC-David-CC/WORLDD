Presentation and input scheduling requirements for the repair
The world management handler currently calls local_view_control synchronously.
That call reads issuer status, builds a projection, obtains broker fences, and
prepares and commits a compositor frame before returning. Component waits pump
movement but do not dispatch normal key batches. The renderer also owns one
in-flight stream, so simply reentering an input call can collide with that owner.

The repair must represent presentation as an owned continuation with bounded
work per scheduler turn. It must allow other actors' input to advance without
sharing mutable stack references across a yield. Retain the exact world, actor,
view, service incarnation, authority generation, projection, and producer frame
identity. Recheck the session, observation rights, broker fences, and producer
identity before each consequential submission and before publishing the frame.
A rejected or cancelled continuation must discard its draft and must not publish
pixels or mutate another actor's state. Handle compositor loss, broker loss,
revocation, shutdown, and a newer selected view as terminal or explicit retry
outcomes. Do not replay uncertain input or silently extend input expiry.

The compositor protocol must serialize its existing stream safely, or provide a
separately authorized input route with independent lifetime and ordering. Merely
moving a blocking call to a thread does not make Actors, broker, or renderer
state thread-safe. A presentation continuation must also coexist with the pending
independent native-seat revocation guardian; it cannot replace that security fix.

Required regressions include the 332-tap test at its unchanged 250-tap threshold,
a key arriving at each presentation phase, unrelated actor movement throughout,
revocation and control transfer at each yield, stale view and frame rejection,
compositor and broker loss, and proof that failed drafts have no visible effect.
