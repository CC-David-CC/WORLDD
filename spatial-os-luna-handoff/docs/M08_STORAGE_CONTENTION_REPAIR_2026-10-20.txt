## Proven cause and remaining uncertainty

The failure bundle proves a workspace issuer transport loss. Broker stderr records `issuer_eof`, phase `reply_header`, elapsed `0 ms`, and `since_previous_exchange_ms=3469`. The configured issuer idle timeout is 3000 ms. `WorkspaceAuthority::keepalive()` marked authority unavailable after that EOF. The workspace then logged `settle_wait_broken` at the same revision, so it could not submit an applied-revision acknowledgement. World and workspace both received revision 1 within about 6 ms. The world logged cancellation settled about 3.4 seconds later. No `ack_queued` event appears.

The sample proves a gap longer than the issuer idle limit and proves that the client treated the resulting EOF as permanent authority failure. It does not yet prove why no keepalive was exchanged during that gap. Do not attribute the loss to the test's six-second deadline or to a service restart. The lifecycle witness shows the same worldd, workspaced, and coordinator PIDs and start ticks before cleanup.

The previous diagnosis at `M08_SESSION_STORAGE_CONTENTION_DIAGNOSIS_2026-10-19.md` suspected synchronous Btrfs sync on the broker loop. That diagnosis used an older source base. At this candidate, `ExecutionStore::filesystem_effect()` runs filesystem sync in `std::async` and calls its observer while waiting (`src/workspace/store.cpp:652-658`). Import routes `sync_tree()` through that wrapper (`store.cpp:1037-1069`). The syscall snapshot nevertheless reports the workspaced PID in `fdatasync` / Btrfs `wait_log_commit`. That conflicts with the current source path. The retained snapshot does not include a thread ID or source operation identity. Resolve this attribution before claiming that import sync blocked the broker thread.

The second host provides a direct witness for the process-stop failure. Its manifest SHA-256 is `2d670c7c9fc03f17c31deb3c47a9f9f1c2907e63d69fc8616b7f958526c15f86`; all eleven listed files match their recorded sizes and hashes. The test captured PIDs `778796-778800` and `778818-778821`. Seven of nine exited within 2.0096 seconds. Broker stderr identifies `pid=778818`, `namespace_pid=778819`, and scope `authority-729553e7638425938be086fd433c08f30cdc45c04c83ac5e81ddaa39f333e7ad`. That scope remained populated and its child unreaped after 2.000642 seconds. `stop_or_exit()` then called `_exit(2)`. The lifecycle witness confirms workspaced exited before fixture cleanup, and the import reply is `Broker authority channel closed`. The retained output does not include a per-PID exit table, so the two logged PIDs are a strong identification of the two still-live witnesses, not a complete per-PID status record.

On sb16, `contention.json` records nine sorted PIDs and only the aggregate exit count: five exited within two seconds. It lacks per-PID cgroup, scope, and exit-time mapping. The four surviving PID values and scopes cannot be recovered. No `application stop unconfirmed` line appears in the retained sb16 broker stderr. The hosts therefore show distinct manifestations: sb16 kept services alive, completed import, then remained fail-closed with both acknowledgements pending; sb16-2 failed one application scope drain, exited workspaced, and returned an import transport error.

Current source shows the cancellation path: the filesystem observer calls `enforce()`; authority changes call `ExecutionStore::reconcile_authority()` (`src/workspace/main.cpp:348-364`). That function erases revoked jobs serially (`src/workspace/store.cpp:1891-1937`). Each `ApplicationProcess` destructor stops and waits for its scope (`src/workspace/process.cpp:407-466`); `ProcessScope::request_stop()` writes `cgroup.kill`, while `complete_stop()` waits for an empty scope and reaps the child (`src/supervisor/process_scope.cpp:1067-1111`). A failed drain makes `stop_or_exit()` terminate the broker. Serial stop-and-drain also delays stop requests to later jobs.

A likely explanation for the sb16 `fdatasync` is process-state persistence in that same reconciliation loop. The execution database uses `PRAGMA synchronous=EXTRA` and `journal_mode=DELETE` (`store.cpp:710-714`). Each `UPDATE processes SET state='revoked'` is an autocommit statement (`store.cpp:1935-1936`). It can synchronously commit through `fdatasync` on the authority thread. This is a strong source-backed explanation for an authority thread in Btrfs `fdatasync` while revocation is reconciled, and for delayed issuer service during a write-limited import. It remains unproven because the captured `/proc` snapshot lacks fd9's target and a thread ID. The sb16-2 workspaced snapshot was in `poll`, so the fdatasync sample cannot be generalized across both hosts.

A control path exists during filesystem effects: the store observer runs while its async filesystem worker waits (`store.cpp:652-658`). Scope-wide kill also exists. The gap is that revocation reconciliation performs stop and durable state work serially on the authority path. A process blocked in uninterruptible I/O may still resist SIGKILL; neither bundle retains that process's kernel stack or `/proc/<pid>/fd` targets. Do not claim a specific I/O wait for PID 778818 or for sb16's four unexited processes.

## Narrow investigation and proposed scope

Proposed narrow production scope, pending primary review: split actor-revocation cancellation from draining. First deny new actions, collect all affected applications, and issue `request_stop()` to every scope. Persist their state changes in one checked durable transaction. Then drain the requested scopes while continuing issuer and control servicing. Keep the participant acknowledgement pending until every process scope is empty and durable reconciliation is complete. A failed commit or populated scope must remain unacknowledged and fail closed. Preserve checked issuer transport, actor-revocation semantics, credential-revocation process survival, and independent grants. Do not raise the issuer idle timeout or any fixture/test deadline. No contract or locked-test edits are proposed.

The source seam is `WorkspaceAuthority::tick()/keepalive()` and the import observer path in `src/workspace/main.cpp`, `src/workspace/store.cpp`, `src/workspace/process.*`, and `src/supervisor/process_scope.*`. Review SQLite transaction behavior and scope identity handling before coding. A diagnostic-only overlay may be needed to retain per-PID scope IDs, `/proc/<pid>/fd` targets, task stacks, and exit timestamps. Do not change product code until primary approves the repair shape.
